Black and white, cut square, with grain.

One look for every lsuite app: ink on paper, nothing rounded, surfaces that cast hard shadows, and a page of film grain and dithered light behind the chrome. The apps are told apart by their names and their work, not by a color. kimchi wears it first; ryolune and zenith follow.

Anatomy of a window

Every area titled, every tool boxed with its kind.

Each area has a title bar like a sidebar's (Media, Viewer, Timeline); tools that belong together sit in one box with hairlines between them, labelled when there is room. What is chosen is inverted. The work stays solid.

Tiers

Three levels, one hard shadow.

Chrome is translucent over the grain; floating and modal surfaces are nearly solid and cast a hard offset shadow, no blur. Dialogs sit inside viewfinder brackets. With "reduce transparency", every tier is opaque.

Marks

One colour, on a near-black tile.

An app's mark is drawn in a single ink with one dithered part, and its icon is that mark in white on a near-black tile with a corner of dithered light. Logos of other services keep their own colours.

kimchi: a napa stalk cut square, a sharp leaf, a leaf dissolving into dither.
Rules

What every app follows.

The accent is ink. White in the dark, black in the light: selection, focus, the playhead, the primary action. A chosen thing is inverted, paper on ink.

Red only for what destroys or records. Every other state is told by an icon or a word, not a hue.

Nothing is rounded. Radii are zero; only things that are round in the world (a sphere, a dial) stay round.

Areas are titled, tools are grouped. A title bar per area; tools boxed with their kind, with labels when there is room.

Grain behind, never on the work. The page is grain and dithered light at device pixels; the canvas, timeline and documents stay solid and clean.

Contrast is tested. Text on every tier passes 4.5:1 over the densest grain, in both modes. Fix the tier, never the threshold.
